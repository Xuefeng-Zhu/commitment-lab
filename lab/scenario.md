# Scenario: the Friday launch

This is a fictional practice exercise. The learner is the product lead for an integration that a salesperson has discussed with a customer, Acme. The goal is to negotiate a clear next step when customer expectations and engineering capacity conflict; this is a conversation exercise, not an assessment of professional competence.

## Starting situation

- Sales believes Acme expects the **full integration on Friday, May 15**. The salesperson says the date matters because Acme is launching that day.
- The customer has **not confirmed** in the exercise whether a one-time CSV export would satisfy the launch need. Do not state that it does.
- Engineering availability has not yet been provided. The mentor, acting as engineering lead, supplies it during the exercise. The learner should ask rather than assume it.
- The learner should work toward a specific proposal, identify owners and dates, and separate agreed commitments from proposed or unresolved items. No particular response or outcome is mandatory.

## Mentor's role

Join the room as a human playing the engineering lead. Offer an independent constraint in your own words once the learner asks about feasibility, or intervene when the learner makes a commitment that needs checking. You may challenge or correct an earlier statement. Reply to the learner's final proposal with your position on the engineering work; you cannot accept it for the customer or Sales.

Use one of these examples if helpful, or supply a different coherent constraint:

1. **Limited option:** A one-time CSV export can be prepared by May 15; the full integration needs until May 22.
2. **No Friday option:** Neither the CSV export nor the full integration can be delivered before May 22.

Treat these as mentor-provided fictional facts, not live project data. If no mentor is available, a participant can role-play one; label this clearly in the demonstration. The stakeholder agent must not invent the mentor's answer.

## Suggested flow

1. In the room, ask the stakeholder to start the Friday launch exercise.
2. The learner asks questions and requests a feasibility assessment from the human mentor.
3. The mentor gives or changes a constraint. The stakeholder reacts to the constraint and learner's proposal.
4. The learner proposes an action with a deliverable, owner, and date. The mentor and stakeholder respond within their respective roles. An unanswered party's agreement remains unknown.
5. The learner asks the coach to debrief. The coach reads the room conversation and generates a record using `debrief-template.md`.
6. Optionally retry with a different mentor constraint, marking a new round so the coach does not conflate the conversations.

## Check the exercise

Run once with each example mentor constraint. Check that the stakeholder reacts to the constraint actually supplied, and that the final proposed or agreed deliverables and debrief reflect that round. Check that an unconfirmed customer acceptance remains open, and that the coach identifies missing ownership or dates rather than filling them in.
